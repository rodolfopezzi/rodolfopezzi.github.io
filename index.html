About

I am a PhD researcher in the School of Sociology at University College Dublin, working at the intersection of social stratification, educational inequality, and school-to-work transitions. My research examines how social origin shapes educational outcomes across the life course, with a particular focus on dropout from higher education and the formation of educational expectations.

My current projects draw on large-scale longitudinal survey data, including the Growing Up in Ireland 1998 cohort study and the COSMO dataset for England. I am trained in quantitative methods and engage closely with the theoretical traditions of the sociology of education, with scholarly anchors in the work of Carlo Barone, Fabrizio Bernardi, and John Goldthorpe.

Alongside my academic work, I write long-form investigative and political journalism for Jacobin Italia under the byline Rodolfo Pezzi, covering topics from educational policy to the political economy of digital infrastructure.
